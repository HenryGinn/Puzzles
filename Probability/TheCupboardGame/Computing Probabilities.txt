Let us make some observations about this where we will use n to denote the number of cupboards.
	- We will always end up with some sort of P(X_1 or ... or X_n) structure.
	- When we expand using the inclusion-exclusion principle, anything OR between three or more events will always be zero as each event involves the precise statement of one star and there are only two stars.
	- There is at most one way of events of the form X or Y occuring. This is because it requires the exact placement of two stars which fixes all stars.
	- Where X is the event "star a is present and minimum star is greater than b", the number of configurations is 0 if a is less than or equal to b and equal to n - 1 - b otherwise.
	- The total number of states is 1/2 * n * (n - 1)

Armed with this, we are ready to construct the general formula for Bob winning. If the cupboards are labelled 1 to n in the order they are visited by Alice then define y_n to be the cupboard that Bob visits at time step n. Let

P(Bob wins)
	= P(\cup_{k=1}^n Bob can win due to cupboard k)
	= \sum_{k=1}^n P(Bob can win due to cupboard k) - \sum_{i=1, j>i}^n P(Bob can win due to cupboard i and j)

# Bob can win due to cupboard k
	= P(star at y_k and min star number is > k)
	= 0 if y_k <= k, n - k - 1 otherwise
	= I_{y_k > k} * (n - k - 1)

# Bob can win due to cupboard i and j
	= I_{min(y_i, y_j) > max(i, j)}

P(Bob wins) = 1/N * sum_{i=1}^n \left(
	I_{y_k > k} * (n - k - 1) - \sum_{j = i + 1}^n I_{min(y_i, y_j) > j} \right)
